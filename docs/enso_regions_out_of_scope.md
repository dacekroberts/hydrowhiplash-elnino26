# El Niño regions this project does not cover

The project studies PNW, BC, AB, Alaska and Yukon, contrasted with NorCal and
SoCal. This file records where else El Niño is known to matter, especially
for fire, so the write-up can say what was left out and why. Not in scope;
no data for these regions is downloaded.

Compiled 2026-10-04 by a research pass (web search and reading). Many
publisher pages returned 403 or a login wall, so several papers were read
only through an abstract, an institutional repository copy or search-result
text. Marks: **[read]** = abstract or full text read; **[search]** = seen in
search results only; **[secondary]** = news or encyclopedia, not
peer-reviewed. Check every source before citing it publicly.

Seasons are those of a developing or mature El Niño (event year 0, ending in
year +1) unless stated.

## Expansion readiness

Added 2026-10-05 (offered 2026-10-04, owner's request via the session 3
handoff). For each region above: how much of this project's pipeline would
carry over if it were added. Planning only; nothing here is downloaded or
in scope. The project's pieces: ERA5-Land monthly and hourly (CC-BY),
ONI/RONI DJF (labelled Y, paired with fire season Y), Natural Earth admin-1
polygons, fire records from MTBS and WFIGS/NIFC (US) and CNFDB (Canada),
station snowpack from NRCS (US west), BC, Yukon and CanSWE (Canada).

Column notes:

- **Climate:** ERA5-Land covers all land on a 0.1° grid; each new area
  needs a download box in `config.ERA5_DOMAINS` (about 50 minutes and 77
  files per box, as the north domain took). Small islands can fall outside
  its land mask.
- **ENSO timing:** "fits" if the fire response follows the DJF winter (the
  project's pairing); "year 0" if fire peaks during the developing El Niño,
  before that winter, which needs a different index season (e.g. JJA or SON
  of year 0), not just new data. Regions driven mainly by another mode note
  it (IOD, Niño 1+2, AMO, AO).
- **Fire data:** whether the project's planned sources cover it. MTBS's
  coverage (all US states and Puerto Rico) is from its programme
  description and its terms are still "to verify" in `DATA_SOURCES.md`.
  Elsewhere a new source would be needed, e.g. a global satellite
  burned-area product, with its own licence read.
- **Snow:** only where snowpack plausibly matters for fire.
- **Pathway:** the project pathway it resembles: *north* (dry or warm
  winter, earlier and drier fire season: dryness-limited), *SoCal* (wet
  year, more fine fuel, fire later: fuel-limited), *reverse* (El Niño wet,
  less fire), or *none*.

**Reading the table.** Three regions are ready on data alone: the US
Southwest (8) and Southeast (9), both *reverse* (El Niño wet, less fire,
the mirror image of PNW/BC/AB), and Hawaii (19), a small *SoCal-like* case.
Adding 8 or 9 would test the project's mechanism with the sign flipped
using the same sources; 10 and 11 have the data but a weak signal. Every
tropical fire region needs a new fire source, and 1, 2 and 12 also a
year-0 index season, which changes the design, not just the inputs.

## Global studies

- **van der Werf et al. 2004**, *Science* 303:73, 10.1126/science.1090753:
  the 1997-98 El Niño fire-emissions anomaly was 2.1 ± 0.8 Pg C, about
  two-thirds of that period's jump in the CO2 growth rate. [search]
- **van der Werf et al. 2017**, *ESSD* 9:697, 10.5194/essd-9-697-2017
  (GFED4s): global fire emissions averaged 2.2 Pg C/yr in 1997-2016, highest
  in 1997 (3.0), lowest in 2013 (1.8). [search]
- **Chen et al. 2017**, *Nat. Clim. Change*, 10.1038/s41558-017-0014-8:
  pan-tropical forest fire emissions 133% higher during and after El Niño
  than La Niña; fire moves in sequence from equatorial Asia (Aug-Oct) to SE
  Asia and northern South America (Jan-Apr), Central America (Mar-May) and
  the southern Amazon (Jul-Oct of year +1). [read, repository]
- **Andela & van der Werf 2014**, *Nat. Clim. Change*, 10.1038/nclimate2313:
  Africa holds about 70% of global burned area; ENSO transitions explain
  about half of southern Africa's 2001-12 burned-area trend. [read,
  repository]
- **Cardil et al. 2023**, *Nat. Commun.* 14:427, 10.1038/s41467-023-36052-8:
  climate teleconnections together modulate 52.9% of global burned area
  (1982-2018), the Tropical North Atlantic mode the strongest. No ENSO-only
  share found; a 2024 comment questions the burned-area dataset used.
  [read, repository]
- **Qu et al. 2025**, *GCB*, 10.1111/gcb.70406: in drylands ENSO acts on
  fire through fuel, with a lag and a negative sign; in the humid tropics
  through weather, with El Niño raising fire. [read, PMC]

No verified single figure exists for ENSO's share of global burned area;
don't quote one.

## Relevance to this project

- **Fuel-limited and dryness-limited systems respond in opposite ways.**
  Grass and savanna systems (southern Africa, northern Australia, Florida
  1998, Hawaii) respond to a wet El Niño year with more fuel and more fire
  later. Forests and peat respond to drought directly. This is the same
  split as the project's SoCal (grass after a wet winter) versus PNW/BC/AB
  (dry winter, low snowpack) pathways.
- **Hoell et al. 2026** (ENSO against extensive area burned by US
  Geographic Area Coordination Centre) covers the project's own regions:
  per its press summary, La Niña raises summer fire risk in Northern
  California and the Great Basin. Worth reading in full before the freeze
  (the paper itself returned 403).
